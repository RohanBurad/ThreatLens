Under the Hood

How ThreatLens detects phishing

A transparent look at every step — from URL input to final verdict. No black box. No guessing.

The Process

4 steps. Instant verdict.

01

URL Parsing & Decomposition

The URL is broken into its components — protocol, subdomain, domain, TLD, path, query parameters, and fragments — using Python's urllib.parse and tldextract libraries. This gives us the raw material for every check that follows.

urllib.parse tldextract regex
02

15+ Heuristic Signal Extraction

Each component is analyzed for known phishing patterns. We check for IP addresses used as hostnames, suspicious TLDs, brand names in wrong positions, obfuscation techniques like hex encoding and @ symbols, excessive subdomain depth, and known phishing keywords in the URL path.

IP Detection Brand Impersonation TLD Analysis Keyword Matching Obfuscation Check
03

SSL Certificate & WHOIS Validation

We open a real SSL handshake to the domain and inspect the certificate — checking validity, issuer, and days remaining. Simultaneously, a WHOIS lookup checks when the domain was first registered. Domains under 30 days old are a major red flag used in temporary phishing campaigns.

Python ssl module python-whois Certificate Expiry Domain Age
04

Weighted Risk Scoring & Verdict

Every signal carries a weight based on its real-world threat relevance. Brand impersonation scores +30, no HTTPS scores +20, a domain under 30 days old scores +25. All weights are summed and capped at 100. The final score maps to one of three verdicts: Safe (0–39), Suspicious (40–69), or Phishing (70–100).
